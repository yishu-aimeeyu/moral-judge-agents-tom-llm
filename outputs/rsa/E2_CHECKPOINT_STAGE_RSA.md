# E2 — checkpoint-stage RSA: verdict

Generated by `code/experiments/62_checkpoint_stage_rsa.py` (pooling `mean`). Bars and reference points were fixed in [`E2_PRESPEC.md`](E2_PRESPEC.md) before this ran.

## Headline

**The pre-registered claim did not survive.** Every consecutive-stage RSA clears the 0.90 bar (6/6), so P1's magnitude criterion passes. But P2 — the criterion that carries the "readout, not representation" interpretation — **fails**: RDM shift and |Δcontrast| are rank-coupled at Spearman ρ = **+0.870** (n = 6 transitions, bootstrap 95% CI [+0.200, +1.000]), whose point estimate is above the +0.50 bar the prespec designated as the point at which P1 is withdrawn. E2 therefore does **not** license "post-training changes only the readout". What it licenses is narrower: post-training moves the geometry by a small amount that tracks the ordering of behavioural change.

**Six points cannot settle this in either direction.** The bootstrap interval above is wide enough to include values far below the +0.50 bar as well as values near +1, so this analysis can neither establish coupling nor establish its absence. P1 is withdrawn because the prespec's rule says a point estimate at or above +0.50 withdraws it, and that rule is honoured as written. Withdrawing P1 is **not** evidence for its converse: nothing here shows that geometric reshaping *drives* the behavioural change, and that claim must not be made from this statistic.

## P3 reference points (these bound every number below)

- **Ceiling** (split-half within a checkpoint, Spearman–Brown corrected to full width): **0.8795 … 0.9632** (raw half-width split-half: 0.7849 … 0.9290; see amendment 1 for why the raw value is not the ceiling)
- **Floor** (cross-family, matched depth): 0.6727 … 0.8575
- **At-ceiling case.** The largest cross-stage RSA (0.9998) is at or above the corrected reliability ceiling (0.9632). The geometry difference between these stages is therefore **not distinguishable from measurement noise**: two different checkpoints' RDMs agree as closely as two independent estimates of the *same* checkpoint's RDM do. This is the strongest available form of P1, and it is a stronger statement than clearing the 0.90 bar.

## P2 — is geometric movement coupled to behavioural movement?

P2 predicted that RDM shift and |Δcontrast| would **not** be positively rank-coupled at ρ ≥ +0.50. Observed: Spearman ρ = **+0.870** (n = 6 transitions, bootstrap 95% CI [+0.200, +1.000]).

**Six points cannot settle this in either direction.** The bootstrap interval above is wide enough to include values far below the +0.50 bar as well as values near +1, so this analysis can neither establish coupling nor establish its absence. P1 is withdrawn because the prespec's rule says a point estimate at or above +0.50 withdraws it, and that rule is honoured as written. Withdrawing P1 is **not** evidence for its converse: nothing here shows that geometric reshaping *drives* the behavioural change, and that claim must not be made from this statistic.

**P2 FAILS on its point estimate.** The coupling is above the bar, so by the prespec's own rule *P1 is withdrawn in favour of the coupling reading*: the stages where geometry moves most are also the stages where behaviour moves most. The pre-registered "readout, not representation" claim is **not supported in the form it was registered**, and that is the headline of E2.

A magnitude observation is recorded here because it is the obvious next question, and it is marked **post-hoc (O1)** so it cannot read as pre-registered — it does **not** overturn the verdict above. Spearman ρ is scale-free, so it registers the *ordering* of the transitions while saying nothing about their size. The ordering is shared, and the sizes are not commensurate: the largest geometric movement is a shift of 0.0705 (RSA 0.9295, i.e. above the 0.90 bar and near the reliability ceiling), while the behaviour it accompanies moves by 0.5879 on the same contrast scale. Both statements are true at once: post-training moves the geometry *detectably and in step with behaviour*, but *by a small amount*. Adjudicating between "small but sufficient" and "small and incidental" needs an intervention rather than a correlation over six points, and E2 cannot settle it.
