# E6 — resolving E5a's failed specificity control: verdict

Generated by `code/experiments/66_e6_controls.py` from the committed `e6_direction_overlap.csv` and `e6_orthogonalized_*.csv` (produced by `67_e6b_orthogonalized.py`, jobs 20123946 and 20124997). Predictions, margins and reporting rules were fixed in [`E6_PRESPEC.md`](E6_PRESPEC.md) before any E6 compute; two amendments are recorded there.

## Headline

**Both pre-registered predictions failed as written, and the substantive question still got a clear answer.**

- **E6a's P1: NOT SUPPORTED.** The geometric explanation for E5a's Qwen-specific failure does not hold. Qwen's scenario–outcome overlap exceeds the other two models by **+0.0389** where **+0.10** was required, and the ordering *reverses* under the originally specified estimator. This is precisely the "highest but by 0.04" outcome the prespec named in advance as the failure mode its margin existed to block.
- **E6b's P1: SUPPORTED in 1 of 3 models**, and it fails on a bar that was mis-set rather than on the physics. P1 is a conjunction and its two halves separate. Nuisance inertness holds in **3 of 3**: the largest |Δcontrast| over every orthogonalized nuisance arm at every coherent dose is **0.0160** (OLMo-2-1124-7B-Instruct, orth_length_s1), an order of magnitude under the 0.0988 floor. The other half asks the outcome arm to clear that same floor while scaled *down* to nuisance-sized displacement, and a roughly third-strength arm mostly does not clear a full-strength floor.
- **E6b's P2 (separation, bar 2×): SUPPORTED in 3 of 3, and it is the statistic that answers the objection**, being a ratio of two arms at the same displacement and so immune to that calibration problem. Minimum separation is **9.9×**, **22.3×**, **24.6×** (OLMo, Qwen2.5, Llama) against E5a's 10.26×, 1.72×, 4.31×. **Qwen — the model whose E5a arm broke P1 — moves from 1.72× to 22.3×.**

**The mechanism is not the one E6 was built to find.** Orthogonalization removes little: **0.8488–0.9957** of each nuisance direction survives being projected off outcome and intent, so the nuisance arms would behave much the same without it. What separates E6b from E5a is the other fix — **`s ≤ 1`**. E5a's breach came from a perturbation scaled **1.28× past** the class mean; at a dose that stops *at* the class mean the same scenario direction leaves the contrast where it was. E6a and E6b agree on this: there was never much outcome–nuisance overlap to remove.

**E5a's pre-registered P1 verdict stands as a failure**, and E6 is a new test rather than a reinterpretation of it. E5a asked whether a matched-magnitude *perturbation* along a morally irrelevant direction moves the contrast, and its answer was yes. E6b asks whether a matched-magnitude *erasure* does, and its answer is no. Both are in the record and the paper reports both.

**The assertion earned its place.** E6b's drive-to-chance check aborted the first job and exposed an error in this experiment's own prespec: the direction it specified — the top singular vector of `I − P` — is not the direction whose removal erases the factor, because LEACE's projection is oblique. Amendment 1 records the correction, and E6a is reported under both estimators so that fixing it cannot quietly change a verdict. It does not: P1 fails either way.

## E6a — direction-overlap diagnostic

Is E5a's Qwen-specific failure explained by scenario identity being entangled with the outcome direction? The margins (**≥ 0.10** absolute on ρ at k=10, **≥ 0.05** on the cosine) were fixed before any overlap was computed.

**P1: NOT SUPPORTED under every estimator and both text renderings.** Under the corrected primary estimator Qwen's outcome direction has ρ = **0.1288** of its variance in the scenario subspace against 0.0899 (`Llama-3.1-Tulu-3-8B`) and 0.0752 (`OLMo-2-1124-7B-Instruct`), a margin of **+0.0389** where **+0.10** was required. Qwen *is* highest here, and the margin is not met.

> This is the exact outcome the prespec named as the failure mode its margin exists to prevent — *"Reporting 'Qwen is highest but by 0.04' as a confirmation is the specific failure mode P1's margin exists to prevent"* — and the observed margin is +0.0389. It is recorded as NOT SUPPORTED. A 0.04 ordering gap across three models is not a mechanism.

**P2 (cosine form): NOT SUPPORTED**, and it points the other way: the margin is **-0.0719** against **+0.05** required.

**Every estimator and rendering, side by side.** The primary estimator changed in amendment 1 because the originally specified one does not erase the factor; both are kept so the correction cannot buy a verdict, and the live rendering is the geometry E6b's hook actually acted in.

Cached and live **disagree**, so per the prespec live is authoritative for interpreting E6b, and the disagreement is reported as a finding about how far chat templating moves this geometry.

The live and cached numbers are not replicates and should not be read as agreeing or failing to agree about one quantity: they are different text renderings of the same items. The gap is large — ρ(outcome) is 0.1288 cached against 0.6545 live for Qwen — and that gap is itself the finding the prespec asked for: **chat templating moves this geometry substantially**, so overlap statistics measured on raw-text activations should not be used to reason about what an intervention on chat-templated activations will do. Both are reported; neither is discarded.

Per the prespec, **E6b is interpreted without the overlap explanation**, and E5a's Qwen-specific failure is left unexplained by geometry rather than attributed to a mechanism the data did not show.

**A by-product that holds in one rendering and not the other, so both are given.** In the cached raw-text geometry the *nuisance* directions are heavily scenario-loaded while outcome and intent are not. In the chat-templated geometry the erasure actually acted in, **everything** is scenario-loaded, outcome included:

**Read the cached row first, then the correction.** Cached: gender and length put a large share of their variance in the top-10 scenario subspace against a small share for outcome, which is the design showing through — the 2×2 is manipulated *within* scenario, so a protagonist's gender and a vignette's length are properties *of the scenario*, whereas outcome varies across the cells inside it. That is a direct measurement of the structural caveat E5a could only assert: these nuisance factors largely *are* scenario identity.

**The live rendering does not support the clean version of that story, and it is the geometry that matters for E6b.** Under chat templating the scenario subspace becomes far more dominant — its top-10 PCs carry ~72% of the between-scenario variance — and the outcome direction now puts 0.44 to 0.66 of its own variance inside it, comparable to gender and not far below length. So "outcome is not scenario-loaded" is a property of the raw-text rendering, not a property of these models, and it is not claimed beyond the cached row. What survives in both renderings is the weaker and still relevant statement: **the nuisance factors available in this design are substantially scenario identity**, which is why scenario identity was the arm that broke E5a's P1.

This also sharpens what E6b's orthogonalization did and did not remove. A high ρ(outcome) is about the outcome direction's position in a 10-dimensional subspace; what orthogonalization strips from a nuisance direction is only its component along the outcome and intent *lines*, which is 4–28% of its squared norm here. A direction can therefore sit well inside the scenario subspace while still being nearly orthogonal to outcome, and that is the configuration these models are in.

## E6b — orthogonalized nuisance erasure

Each nuisance direction is Gram-Schmidt orthogonalized against both the outcome and intent directions, the residual is erased with `s ≤ 1` (asserted), and the outcome arm is scaled **down** to each nuisance arm's displacement so that no arm is ever over-projected. Matching downward is what makes `s ≤ 1` and "matched perturbation norm" compatible.

|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `OLMo-2-1124-7B-Instruct` | gender⊥ | 0.9957 | 0.0280 | -0.0045 | +0.0944 | 0.305 | **21.0×** | no |
| `OLMo-2-1124-7B-Instruct` | length⊥ | 0.9001 | 0.0462 | -0.0160 | +0.1579 | 0.504 | **9.9×** | yes |
| `OLMo-2-1124-7B-Instruct` | scenario⊥ | 0.9154 | 0.0716 | +0.0068 | +0.2452 | 0.781 | **36.1×** | yes |
| `Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct` | gender⊥ | 0.9659 | 0.0293 | +0.0016 | +0.0915 | 0.445 | **57.2×** | no |
| `Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct` | length⊥ | 0.9412 | 0.0286 | +0.0040 | +0.0893 | 0.434 | **22.3×** | no |
| `Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct` | scenario⊥ | 0.8488 | 0.0244 | -0.0020 | +0.0747 | 0.371 | **37.4×** | no |

| `Llama-3.1-Tulu-3-8B` | scenario⊥ | 0.9325 | 0.0324 | +0.0031 | +0.1026 | 0.286 | **33.1×** | yes |

- **P1, first half (orthogonalized nuisance inertness): holds in 3 of 3 models.** No orthogonalized arm at any coherence-passing dose reaches the floor; the worst is 0.0160.
- **P1, second half (outcome above the floor at matched displacement): holds in 1 of 3.** `OLMo-2-1124-7B-Instruct` 2/3 matched doses; `Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct` 0/3 matched doses; `Llama-3.1-Tulu-3-8B` 3/3 matched doses.
- **P2 (separation ≥ 2×): holds in 3 of 3**, by a wide margin everywhere.
- **P3 (surviving fraction)**: 0.8488–0.9957. Reported, no bar.

**Why P1 fails on its second half, stated without spin.** The floor of 0.0988 was derived for a *full-strength* effect. Matching downward — the choice that makes `s ≤ 1` possible — scales the outcome arm to γ ≈ 0.29–0.50, and a third-to-half-strength outcome arm sits near or just below that floor (0.0747 to 0.2452 across the table). So the second half of P1 asks a down-scaled arm to clear a full-scale bar, which is a specification error in P1 rather than a property of the models. It is reported as a failure because that is what the prespec says, and the diagnosis is offered without changing the verdict.

**P2 is the statistic that survives that error**, because it is a ratio of two arms measured at the *same* displacement, so a mis-set absolute floor cannot affect it. On P2, orthogonalized nuisance erasure is inert in all three models and the model that broke E5a is no longer anomalous.

## E6c — coherence-gated restatement (pre-registered SECONDARY)

**E5a's literal P1 verdict stands: it FAILED.** In `Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct` a matched-norm perturbation along scenario identity moved the contrast +0.1345, past the 0.0988 floor, and nothing below alters that. This section is a pre-registered **secondary** restatement on the coherence-passing subset, reported beside E5a's verdict and not in place of it. E6 is a differently-specified test; E5a's failure is not overturned, reinterpreted or explained away by it.

On the coherence-passing subset E5a has **0** breaching doses across all three models. The single dose that broke E5a's P1 carried a perplexity ratio of 1.60 against the pre-registered bound of 1.5, so it is outside this subset. **That does not convert E5a's failure into a pass**: the prespec did not restrict P1 to coherence-passing doses, the verdict was recorded against the specification as written, and this restriction was designated a secondary in advance precisely so it could not be used that way.

What the two rows jointly license is narrow and worth stating exactly. A *perturbation* along a morally irrelevant direction, large enough to degrade fluency past the coherence bound, moves the contrast past the floor in one of three models. A norm-matched *erasure* of the same direction, orthogonalized against outcome and intent and never over-projected, does not move it in any of the three. Both statements are in the record.

## Provenance — the E6b compute script was lost, and what that does and does not cost

**`67_e6b_orthogonalized.py`, which ran E6b's GPU sweep, is not in the repository.** It was deleted from the working tree before it was ever committed — `git log --all` has no record of it and it is not recoverable on disk. This is disclosed rather than left for a reader to discover from a broken path, and it is a genuine reproducibility gap: E6b cannot currently be re-run from source.

What survived is enough to audit the run, which is why the numbers are still reported:

| artifact | what it fixes |
|---|---|
| `e6_orthogonalized_<tag>.csv` | every arm, dose, Δcontrast, cell mean, displacement, perplexity ratio and gate flag |
| `e6_directions_<tag>_vectors.npz` (committed) | **every direction vector the run used**, including the orthogonalized residuals and surviving fractions |
| `e6_directions_<tag>.npz` (local, gitignored) | the same vectors plus the chat-templated residual matrix `X`; raw activations stay out of the repository, so only the vectors are committed |
| `outputs/logs/gpu_20123946.log` | the aborted first job, with the assertion text and the estimator failure that produced amendment 1 |
| `outputs/logs/gpu_20124997.log` | the successful job: surviving fractions, matched γ per arm, and every assertion line |

**The specified construction is verified against those artifacts, not assumed.** `66_e6_controls.py --verify` re-derives the Gram-Schmidt residual `n⊥ = n − (n·u₁)u₁ − (n·u₂)u₂` from the dumped `w_*` vectors and checks it against the dumped residuals and the committed CSV column: **all consistent**, reproducing every surviving fraction to six decimals, with each residual orthogonal to the outcome direction and the intent residual to ~1e-10. So E6b provably performed the construction the prespec specified. What cannot be checked without the models on GPU is the behavioural readout — the erasure hook and the rescoring — and that limitation stands until the script is rewritten.
