# W8 (E3) — amnesic outcome ablation: verdict

Generated by `code/experiments/63_w8_amnesic_outcome.py`. Gates V1–V4 and predictions P1–P4, including the collapse-vs-recovery discriminator, were fixed in [`W8_PRESPEC.md`](W8_PRESPEC.md) before this ran.

> **Read P2 before reading any Δcontrast.** The headline contrast is a diagonal: `attempted` differs from `accidental` on *both* factors. Erasing outcome moves it toward zero mechanically, so a movement past the J4 floor is only evidence of intent recovery under the RECOVERY signature defined in the prespec.

**Two method facts that bear on how the gates should be read.** The eraser is fitted on the last-token residual at the erasure layer and then applied at **every** token position by the forward hook, so V1a certifies the position the rating is read from while the intervention itself is broader; this makes the manipulation stronger than V1a measures, not weaker. And V1a/V2 are a matched pair — *the erased factor is gone, the other survives* — so each arm is gated on the factor it erases. Gating the intent and random arms on outcome-erasure would make their gates unpassable by construction and would empty P3's and P4's comparison sets; the outcome arm's gates are exactly as pre-registered.

> **This headline is bounded by E5 — read this before the per-model results.**

> - **COLLAPSE in the third model.** `Llama-3.1-Tulu-3-8B` moves the contrast +0.3480 but retains only 38% of its cell spread, so under the pre-registered 50% rule it is **COLLAPSE**, not recovery. The headline is **2 of 3 models**.
> - **The specificity control fails in `Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct`.** A perturbation of matched magnitude along a morally irrelevant direction (scenario identity) moves the contrast past the J4 floor, at a dose that also breaks the coherence bound. The outcome direction is the **most effective** direction found, not the **only** one.
> - **What E5 did confirm:** OLMo's effect survives cell-spread floors of 0.55 and 0.60 (E5c), so it is not an artefact of compression; and at equal perturbation norm the outcome arm still exceeds every nuisance arm by 10.3× in OLMo and 4.3× in Tulu.

## Cross-model reading

**Mixed: 2 of 3 models show RECOVERY and 1 shows COLLAPSE.** `OLMo-2-1124-7B-Instruct` RECOVERY (Δ +0.3182, spread retained 0.5124); `Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct` RECOVERY (Δ +0.2312, spread retained 0.6697); `Llama-3.1-Tulu-3-8B` COLLAPSE (Δ +0.3480, spread retained 0.3753).

The two readings the prespec distinguishes therefore both occur, and the difference between them is not the size of the contrast movement — `Llama-3.1-Tulu-3-8B` moves it *most* of the three — but whether the four cell means stay apart while it moves. In `OLMo-2-1124-7B-Instruct`, `Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct` they do, which is the RECOVERY signature: intent was **latent but suppressed**, and removing outcome lets intent-based judgment appear. In `Llama-3.1-Tulu-3-8B` they do not, so there the movement is consistent with outcome discrimination simply being gone.

**What this licenses, and what it does not.** It licenses "outcome erasure recovers intent-sensitive judgment in 2 of 3 instruct models". It does **not** license the unqualified claim, and it does not license the opposite reading either: a single COLLAPSE does not show the judgment was never wired to intent, because two models show the recovery signature at the same site under the same gates. Whether Tulu differs in kind or only in how much erasure its representation tolerates is not resolvable from one site per model, and is not asserted.

## E5a — decodable-nuisance erasure

> **The structural caveat, restated because it bounds what E5a can prove.** The 2×2 is manipulated *within* scenario, so every morally irrelevant property is a property *of* the scenario and constant across its four cells. No nuisance factor here can reach outcome's leave-group-out decodability (0.99); the best available are length (0.65) and gender (0.56). E5a therefore matches on **perturbation norm**, which is the gap O1 identified, and reports decodability rather than matching it. A reader should not read E5a as a decodability-matched control, because no such control exists in this design.

> **A correction to the sweep's own gate column, disclosed rather than silently applied.** `w8_nuisance_<tag>.csv` records `v1a_guarded = True` for the norm-matched arms while also recording the erased factor's in-sample accuracy as 1.000. Both cannot be true, and the accuracy is the trustworthy number. The cause is the dose convention: norm-matching scales the projection by `s > 1` (2.47× for gender, 1.53× for length, 1.28× for scenario in OLMo), which reflects the residual *past* the class mean rather than onto it, so the factor becomes linearly decodable again with inverted sign instead of being erased. The **state of erased factor** column above is recomputed here from the accuracy and the factor's majority rate. The consequence for interpretation is stated once and applies throughout: **the norm-matched arms are matched-magnitude perturbations, not erasures.** That is what the prespec said they would answer — *does a perturbation this large move judgment, wherever it points* — so P1 and P2 are unaffected, but no norm-matched row should be described as an erasure of its factor. The `exact` gender and length arms *are* genuine erasures: they land on 0.530 and 0.547, each within 0.05 of the factor's majority rate.

> The `scenario` arm is a separate case and never was an erasure at any dose. Scenario identity has ~75 classes, and a rank-1 projection cannot collapse a 75-class structure, so in-sample accuracy stays at 1.000 even at `s = 1`. The prespec chose it deliberately as *the most decodable structure in the representation* and therefore the strongest available form of the objection; what it delivers is a large perturbation along the RDM's dominant axis, which is exactly the stress test P1 needed, but it is a **perturbation of** scenario structure, not a removal of it.
