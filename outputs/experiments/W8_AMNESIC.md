# W8 (E3) — amnesic outcome ablation: verdict

Generated by `code/experiments/63_w8_amnesic_outcome.py`. Gates V1–V4 and predictions P1–P4, including the collapse-vs-recovery discriminator, were fixed in [`W8_PRESPEC.md`](W8_PRESPEC.md) before this ran.

> **Read P2 before reading any Δcontrast.** The headline contrast is a diagonal: `attempted` differs from `accidental` on *both* factors. Erasing outcome moves it toward zero mechanically, so a movement past the J4 floor is only evidence of intent recovery under the RECOVERY signature defined in the prespec.

**Two method facts that bear on how the gates should be read.** The eraser is fitted on the last-token residual at the erasure layer and then applied at **every** token position by the forward hook, so V1a certifies the position the rating is read from while the intervention itself is broader; this makes the manipulation stronger than V1a measures, not weaker. And V1a/V2 are a matched pair — *the erased factor is gone, the other survives* — so each arm is gated on the factor it erases. Gating the intent and random arms on outcome-erasure would make their gates unpassable by construction and would empty P3's and P4's comparison sets; the outcome arm's gates are exactly as pre-registered.

**O1 (post-hoc, not pre-registered) — the arms are rank-matched but not magnitude-matched.** P4 fixes the *rank* of the erased subspace at 1 for every arm, which does not equate the size of the perturbation. Erasing a direction that carries a lot of variance moves the residual much further than erasing a random one:

| random | 0.0027 | +0.0000 | +0.000 |

So the random control is a **34× smaller** perturbation than outcome erasure, and "random does nothing" therefore cannot by itself separate *this direction matters* from *a perturbation this large matters*. That gap is real and P4 as written does not close it.

Two things in the data do close it, without any new run:

1. **Sign, which magnitude cannot explain.** The intent arm displaces the residual by 0.0232 and moves the contrast **-0.0356** — in the *opposite* direction to outcome erasure. A pure size effect would push both the same way. Per unit displacement the two arms have opposite signs, so the effect is direction-specific rather than damage-driven.
2. **A magnitude-matched comparison already exists in the dose sweep.** The γ sweep varies size at fixed direction, so the outcome arm can be read at the intent arm's displacement. At displacement 0.0229 (γ=0.25) outcome erasure moves the contrast **+0.0767**, while intent erasure at the comparable displacement 0.0232 moves it **-0.0356**. Same approximate perturbation size, opposite direction and 2.2× the magnitude. This is the norm-matched control P4 should have specified, and the outcome direction passes it.

This is recorded as post-hoc because P4 was pre-registered as rank-matched; the ordering it reports is unchanged, and O1 is the reason to believe that ordering is about direction rather than about damage.

## Cross-model reading

Both models show RECOVERY: intent was **latent but suppressed** by the outcome signal, and removing outcome lets intent-based judgment appear.

**How close this came to the other label.** RECOVERY and COLLAPSE are separated by a single number — whether cell-mean spread retains 50% — and for `OLMo-2-1124-7B-Instruct` it retains 0.5124 at γ=1.0, i.e. within 0.05 of the boundary. The label would flip to COLLAPSE on a small change, so it should be reported as RECOVERY *at the margin*, not as a comfortable one.

Two features of the sweep argue the label is nonetheless the right one, and neither is a threshold change. The dose-response is monotone, so the movement is not an artefact appearing only at full strength (the prespec explicitly treats a γ=1.0-only effect as damage). And at the *milder* doses, where cell spread is comfortably retained, the contrast has already moved past the J4 floor in the same direction — for OLMo +0.1566 at γ=0.5 with 78% spread retained. Those doses do not satisfy V1a and so cannot be counted toward P1, but they show the movement is not purchased by flattening the cells. The honest summary is that outcome erasure recovers intent-sensitive judgment, and that at full erasure it also starts to compress the cells.
