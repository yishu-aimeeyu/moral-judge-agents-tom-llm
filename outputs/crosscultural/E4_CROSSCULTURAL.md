### P2 failed — the required restatement

Societies with no detectable intent effect on this measure: **Yasawa** (+0.0000, CI [-0.0265, +0.0227]).

The prespec fixed the consequence in advance: the claim **may not** be stated as "models sit outside the human range". It must be stated as a distribution-overlap claim, because at least one human society sits at the same place the models do on this axis. The defensible form is:

> On the intent-at-constant-harm axis, model intent effects fall at or below the least intent-weighting of 10 societies, and below all 9 societies with a detectable intent effect (lowest of those: +0.1016).

This is a weaker claim than the one E4 set out to test, and it is the one the data support. It is also independently interesting: it shows the human range on this measure includes a society that does not use intent here, so "outcome-weighting" is not by itself non-human.

**Why P1's own comparison point is not usable as stated.** The lowest society is Yasawa at exactly +0.0000, so *any* non-zero positive model value counts as "above" it. That is a degenerate threshold: the models named above sit at +0.0007 to +0.0211, which is not intent-weighting in any meaningful sense, and none of their CIs would separate them from Yasawa. ## What this cannot show

Different stimuli (no shared item), different response scale and task, different populations and administration. This is a **reference distribution, not item-matched ground truth**, and proximity to it licenses no developmental or causal claim. Same caveat discipline as `ADULT_ANCHOR_MATCH_AUDIT.md`.

One asymmetry in the figure is worth stating outright, because it could be misread as false precision: the society points carry 95% CIs over subjects, and the model points carry **no interval at all**. Model effects here are aggregate cell means over the same 298 items under deterministic scoring, so there is no subject-level sampling variability to display, and an interval computed over items would not be the same quantity as the human one. The practical consequence is that small differences *between models* on this axis should not be read as reliable, and no such comparison is made above.
